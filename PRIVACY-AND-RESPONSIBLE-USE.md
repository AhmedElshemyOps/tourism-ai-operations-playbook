# Privacy and Responsible Use

## Purpose

This policy establishes the minimum responsible-use standard for the Tourism AI Operations Playbook.

## Information that should not be entered into public AI tools

- full guest names when not necessary;
- passport, identity or visa-document copies;
- payment-card or bank information;
- private telephone numbers and email addresses without a valid purpose;
- confidential supplier rates, contracts or commercial terms;
- employee records or performance information;
- passwords, API keys, access tokens or security procedures;
- internal incident details that could identify a person or company;
- copyrighted documents that the user is not authorised to upload.

Use anonymised, fictional or approved data whenever possible.

## Human accountability

AI output is a draft. The accountable professional must verify the output against approved systems, official sources, current supplier confirmations, SOPs and management instructions before use.

## Dynamic tourism information

Always verify information that can change, including attraction hours, prices, ticket conditions, visa rules, transport times, airline schedules, events, road restrictions, weather, accessibility and supplier availability.

## Bias and cultural sensitivity

Review outputs for cultural assumptions, stereotypes, inappropriate language, accessibility barriers and destination-specific sensitivities. Multilingual communication should be reviewed by a competent speaker when the consequence of error is significant.

## Incident reporting

If an AI output creates or contributes to a guest, operational, commercial, privacy or reputational problem:

1. stop using the affected output;
2. protect the guest and operation;
3. preserve the prompt, input, output and review record;
4. notify the accountable manager;
5. assess privacy or security impact;
6. document corrective and preventive actions;
7. update the prompt or governance rule before reuse.

## Legal note

This repository provides operational guidance and training material, not legal advice. Organisations should obtain qualified legal, privacy, cybersecurity and regulatory guidance where required.
